Debunking Basics and Natural Explanations

A field-method paper on elimination, testing, and responsible unresolved findings.

Evidence Science and Analysis Investigation Science

Abstract

This paper frames debunking as an evidence-preserving process, not a dismissive attitude. The goal is to test ordinary causes carefully before describing a finding as unresolved.

Field Context

Most field claims occur inside active environments: buildings move, HVAC systems cycle, plumbing knocks, animals travel through walls, traffic throws light, and people misperceive under stress. These ordinary sources are part of the field context, not an interruption of it.

Motivation and Bias

Bias appears on both sides. A believer may resist ordinary explanations, while a debunker may accept a weak explanation too quickly. Both shortcuts damage the case. The standard is documented elimination, not personal preference.

Applied Method

List possible natural sources, test what can be tested, attempt reproduction, compare timing, document negative results, and preserve uncertainty where the evidence is incomplete. Explained, likely explained, unresolved, and unusable should remain separate categories.

Conclusion

Debunking is strongest when it is specific. Naming the pipe, vent, reflection, device artifact, or human source is better than waving away a claim. If the cause cannot be established, the honest finding is unresolved, not paranormal by default.
