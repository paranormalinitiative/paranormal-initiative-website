Basic Terminology and Foundational Language

A terminology paper for using careful language before claims become conclusions.

Reporting and Documentation Community Development and Publication

Abstract

This paper explains why shared terminology matters in paranormal research. Clear terms help teams distinguish claims, observations, interpretations, evidence, and conclusions.

Field Context

The field uses language from folklore, religion, science, media, psychical research, technology, and team tradition. Words such as EVP, anomaly, residual, intelligent, debunked, evidence, and proof are often used inconsistently.

Motivation and Bias

Terminology can smuggle conclusions into the record. Calling a sound a spirit voice, a shadow an apparition, or a location demonic can shape the case before review. Vague terms also let teams sound certain without being precise.

Applied Method

Define working terms in each report, use descriptive labels before causal labels, separate witness language from investigator language, and connect specialized terms to documented observations whenever possible.

Conclusion

Clear terminology is a form of evidence care. It keeps the case reviewable and prevents language from doing work that the evidence has not earned.
