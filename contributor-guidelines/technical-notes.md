# Technical Notes

Technical notes are shorter focused papers about tools, devices, software, sensors, workflows, file handling, AI use, media review, or documentation systems.

Technical notes are different from research notes. A technical note explains how a tool, system, process, or technical issue works. A research note preserves an early idea, question, observation, or developing line of thought.

## Purpose

A technical note should explain how something works, what it measures, how it should be used, what can go wrong, and what investigators should remember when interpreting the result.

Technical notes are useful when the topic does not need a full research paper but still needs careful explanation.

## Expected Style

Write clearly and practically. The reader should come away knowing what the tool or process does and what it does not do.

The tone should be:

- plain-language first
- technically accurate
- practical
- careful with limitations
- useful in the field

Avoid making equipment sound more powerful or mysterious than it is.

## Recommended Structure

A technical note may include:

- Title
- Tool, method, or system being discussed
- Plain-language summary
- What it does
- What it does not do
- Common uses
- Common mistakes
- Setup or workflow notes
- Limitations
- Example field language
- Author note

## Media and Supporting Material

Technical notes may include:

- screenshots
- device photos
- diagrams
- signal-flow charts
- workflow graphics
- settings examples
- tables
- comparison charts
- short video demonstrations

Visuals should make the technical explanation easier to understand.

## Good Fit Examples

- What EMF meters actually measure
- How to preserve original audio files
- Difference between waveform and spectrogram views
- Using AI responsibly in research notes
- Camera compression artifacts
- ACS source-material transparency

## What To Avoid

- Calling a tool a spirit detector
- Treating a sensor reading as proof
- Using technical terms without explaining them
- Overloading the reader with unnecessary detail
- Recommending unsafe or untested practices
