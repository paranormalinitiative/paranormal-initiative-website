# Review Papers

Review papers summarize, compare, or evaluate existing ideas, books, articles, methods, devices, theories, historical claims, or research areas.

## Purpose

A review paper should help readers understand what has already been said or tried. It should compare viewpoints, identify useful ideas, explain disagreements, and clarify what remains unresolved.

Review papers are especially useful when a topic has many claims, sources, or competing interpretations.

## Expected Style

Write as a guide, not a verdict. A review paper should help readers think through a subject carefully.

The tone should be:

- balanced
- source-aware
- analytical
- readable
- fair to differing viewpoints
- clear about uncertainty

## Recommended Structure

A review paper may include:

- Title
- Topic or question being reviewed
- Why the topic matters
- Sources, authors, methods, or ideas being compared
- Summary of major positions
- Strengths
- Weaknesses or limitations
- Open questions
- Conclusion
- References or source links
- Author note

## Media and Supporting Material

Review papers may include:

- comparison tables
- timelines
- charts
- source lists
- diagrams
- book or article citations
- screenshots when allowed
- links to external sources

Use media to clarify comparison, not to overwhelm the reader.

## Good Fit Examples

- Comparing EVP review methods
- Reviewing historical ITC claims
- Summarizing common haunting classifications
- Comparing different approaches to evidence review
- Reviewing equipment claims and limitations
- Reviewing AI use in paranormal research support

## What To Avoid

- Copying large sections of outside works
- Attacking authors or teams personally
- Treating one source as final authority
- Ignoring limitations or counterarguments
- Presenting a summary as original experimental proof
