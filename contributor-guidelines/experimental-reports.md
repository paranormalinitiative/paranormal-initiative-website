# Experimental Reports

Experimental reports document a test, session, device trial, EVP or ITC experiment, environmental observation, or field method in a way that another reader can understand and review.

## Purpose

An experimental report should explain what was tested, how it was tested, what was observed, what controls were used, and what the limits of the result are.

The goal is not to prove a paranormal claim. The goal is to document an experiment clearly enough that the work can be understood, repeated, questioned, or improved.

## Expected Style

Write plainly and precisely. Experimental reports should be practical, transparent, and honest about uncertainty.

The tone should be:

- direct
- method-focused
- organized
- cautious
- repeatable when possible
- honest about problems or contamination

Avoid dramatic language or conclusions that go beyond the experiment.

## Recommended Structure

An experimental report may include:

- Title
- Experiment type
- Purpose or research question
- Date, time, and location if relevant
- Equipment or software used
- Setup
- Procedure
- Controls
- Observations
- Results
- Limitations
- Conclusion
- Suggested follow-up
- Author note

## Media and Supporting Material

Experimental reports may include:

- photos of the setup
- screenshots of software settings
- device images
- audio clips
- video clips
- spectrograms
- waveform images
- charts or graphs
- environmental logs
- tables of session data

Every media item should be labeled clearly. If an audio, video, or image example is included, explain its source, time, context, and limitations.

## Controls and Documentation

Good experimental reports should describe:

- original files preserved
- copies used for review
- device settings
- baseline conditions
- nearby contamination sources
- human speech or movement
- environmental conditions
- what could not be controlled

If the experiment had weak controls, say so. Weak controls do not make the report useless if they are honestly documented.

## Good Fit Examples

- ACS EVP session report
- Spirit box contamination test
- K2 meter and mobile phone distance test
- Water ITC session documentation
- Audio recorder placement comparison
- Environmental baseline observation

## What To Avoid

- Saying a device response proves communication
- Posting a clip without session context
- Hiding failed attempts
- Editing media without explaining what was changed
- Treating an experiment as stronger than its controls allow
