# Field Articles

Field articles are readable pieces based on investigation experience, haunted locations, team practice, public education, equipment habits, witness interaction, or lessons learned in the field.

## Purpose

A field article should help readers understand a practical issue in paranormal investigation. It can be more conversational than a research paper, but it should still be thoughtful, useful, and responsible.

Field articles are ideal for public-facing education and investigator development.

## Expected Style

Write like you are explaining the subject to another investigator or curious reader. The style can be human and approachable, but it should not become sensational or careless.

The tone should be:

- clear
- practical
- respectful
- experience-informed
- educational
- grounded

## Recommended Structure

A field article may include:

- Title
- Short introduction
- Main issue or question
- Field context
- Practical examples
- What investigators should watch for
- Common mistakes
- Takeaway or conclusion
- Author note

## Media and Supporting Material

Field articles may include:

- field photos
- location images
- equipment photos
- short video embeds
- diagrams
- simple checklists
- examples of notes or logs
- social post graphics when appropriate

Images should support the field lesson. If a photo shows a location, equipment setup, or example condition, explain what the reader should notice.

## Good Fit Examples

- Why baseline readings matter
- Not every orb is the same
- Why investigations fail before they begin
- What to look for during a walkthrough
- How to talk to witnesses respectfully
- Why haunted locations matter

## What To Avoid

- Turning field advice into personal bragging
- Using fear-based wording
- Mocking beginners or other teams
- Presenting opinion as fact
- Making a location claim stronger than the evidence supports
