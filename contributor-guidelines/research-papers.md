# Research Papers

Research papers are full-length written works that examine a paranormal topic, method, theory, evidence issue, historical question, or research area in a serious and organized way.

## Purpose

A research paper should help readers understand a subject more clearly. It should explain the topic, give context, examine different angles, identify limits, and present a careful conclusion.

Research papers are best for topics that need more depth than a short article can provide.

## Expected Style

Write in a scholarly but readable voice. The paper may be technical where needed, but it should still be understandable to a thoughtful reader who may not already know the field.

The tone should be:

- serious
- clear
- respectful
- evidence-conscious
- careful with claims
- open to questions and limits

Avoid sensational language, fear-based claims, ridicule, personal attacks, or unsupported certainty.

## Recommended Structure

A research paper may include:

- Title
- Subtitle or short description
- Introduction
- Background or context
- Key terms or definitions
- Main discussion
- Evidence, examples, sources, or observations
- Limitations
- Conclusion
- Author note
- References or source links when appropriate

The exact headings can change depending on the subject. The paper should read like one complete article, not a collection of separate cards.

## Media and Supporting Material

Research papers may include:

- photographs
- diagrams
- charts
- tables
- screenshots
- historical images when allowed
- audio or video embeds
- links to source material

Images, graphs, and tables should support the paper, not decorate it. If a visual is used, explain why it matters.

## Claim Language

Use careful language such as:

- possible
- plausible
- unresolved
- appears to suggest
- may indicate
- requires further review
- cannot be determined from this material alone

Avoid claiming proof unless the evidence and context can genuinely support that level of certainty.

## Good Fit Examples

- What is paranormal investigation?
- EVP and ITC review language
- Historical development of ghost hunting
- Environmental causes of reported activity
- Ethics and witness care in investigations
- The role of documentation in evidence review

## What To Avoid

- Short opinion posts with no context
- Personal stories presented as proof
- Claims that cannot be checked or explained
- Overly academic wording that hides the point
- Copying full material from other authors without permission
