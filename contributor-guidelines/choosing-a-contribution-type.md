# Choosing a Contribution Type

The Paranormal Initiative accepts several kinds of serious written work. The category should be chosen by the main purpose of the piece, not by how important the topic feels.

If a contribution crosses categories, choose the closest primary type and use labels for the secondary subjects.

## Quick Guide

| If the piece mainly does this | Use this type |
| --- | --- |
| Explains a topic in depth | Research Paper |
| Documents a test, session, trial, or method | Experimental Report |
| Explains a tool, process, device, software issue, or workflow | Technical Note |
| Preserves an early idea, question, observation, or short documented thought | Research Note |
| Shares field experience, investigation practice, or lessons learned | Field Article |
| Studies a location, claim, case history, or background record | Case or Location Study |
| Compares sources, methods, theories, equipment, or claims | Review Paper |
| Examines audio, photo, video, screenshots, files, timestamps, or possible contamination | Media Review |

## Review Paper, Not Peer Review

Use Review Paper for work that reviews books, articles, methods, equipment, claims, theories, historical material, or existing research.

Peer review is different. Peer review means a formal review process where qualified reviewers evaluate a submission before publication. The Paranormal Initiative may develop that kind of process later, but ordinary contributor submissions should not be labeled peer-reviewed unless that review has actually happened.

## Mixed Contributions

Some work will naturally overlap.

An ACS session may be both an experimental report and a technical note. A haunted location write-up may include field observations, history, photographs, witness accounts, and evidence review. A review paper may also include the author's own experience.

That is fine. Choose the category that best describes the center of the article, then use labels to identify the other subjects.

## Labels

Labels are useful for subjects and themes. They should not replace the contribution type.

Possible labels include:

- EVP
- ITC
- ACS
- equipment
- field investigation
- evidence review
- media review
- ethics
- methodology
- consciousness
- anomalous experience
- environmental factors
- location history
- folklore
- technical process

## Plain Rule

If the work is clear, documented, honest about limits, and useful to readers, it belongs somewhere in the system. The category exists to help people find it.
