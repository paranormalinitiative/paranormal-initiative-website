# Research Notes

Research notes are shorter written contributions used for developing ideas, early observations, working questions, field reflections, source notes, or documented thoughts that are not ready to become a full research paper.

## Purpose

A research note should preserve an idea or observation clearly enough that it can be remembered, discussed, expanded, tested, or connected to later work.

Research notes are useful when something matters but does not yet need the structure of a full paper or experimental report.

## Expected Style

Write clearly and honestly. A research note may be exploratory, but it should still explain what prompted the note and what limits should be kept in mind.

The tone should be:

- thoughtful
- concise
- documented
- open-ended
- careful with uncertainty
- useful for future review

Avoid making a research note sound more final than it is.

## Recommended Structure

A research note may include:

- Title
- Note type or topic
- Date or context if relevant
- Observation, question, or idea
- Why it matters
- Supporting details
- Images, graphs, screenshots, audio, video, or source links when useful
- Limits or concerns
- Possible next steps
- Author note

The structure can stay simple. The important part is clarity.

## Media and Supporting Material

Research notes may include:

- field photographs
- screenshots
- short audio or video examples
- charts
- quick graphs
- tables
- diagrams
- source links
- equipment settings
- environmental notes

Media should be labeled enough that another reader understands where it came from and why it is included.

## Good Fit Examples

- A question raised during EVP review
- A short note about a repeating equipment issue
- A developing thought about consciousness and anomalous experience
- A field observation that may deserve later testing
- A source note about an article, book, video, or lecture
- A short comparison between two possible explanations

## What To Avoid

- Treating early observations as proof
- Posting vague claims without context
- Using research notes as a place for unsupported certainty
- Including private case details without permission
- Copying outside material instead of summarizing, linking, or citing responsibly
