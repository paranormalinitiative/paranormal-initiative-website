# Case and Location Studies

Case and location studies examine a specific location, claim, report, investigation, historic site, or case pattern with context and documentation.

## Purpose

A case or location study should organize what is known about a place or claim. It should bring together history, reported experiences, environmental context, field observations, evidence review, and limitations.

These studies should help readers understand the location or claim without turning it into legend-building.

## Expected Style

Write with respect for the location, witnesses, owners, and history. The study should feel careful and documented, not promotional or sensational.

The tone should be:

- respectful
- historical where needed
- evidence-conscious
- balanced
- clear about what is known and unknown

## Recommended Structure

A case or location study may include:

- Title
- Location or case overview
- Public/private status
- Historical background
- Reported claims
- Witness accounts when allowed
- Environmental or structural context
- Investigation notes
- Evidence or media review
- Limitations
- Conclusion
- Author note
- Sources or references

Private residential cases must protect client identity and sensitive details unless written permission exists.

## Media and Supporting Material

Case and location studies may include:

- location photos
- historic photos when permitted
- maps
- floor plans or layout sketches
- timelines
- tables of claims
- environmental logs
- audio or video clips
- evidence review screenshots
- source links

Do not use images or private information that violate permission, privacy, copyright, or site rules.

## Good Fit Examples

- A haunted location historical study
- A cemetery folklore and record review
- A residential case pattern summary with identifying details removed
- A historic building claim review
- A location study comparing claims to documented history

## What To Avoid

- Naming private clients without permission
- Publishing sensitive addresses
- Presenting folklore as verified history
- Using dramatic claims to promote a location
- Ignoring ordinary environmental or structural explanations
