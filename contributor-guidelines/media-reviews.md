# Media Reviews

Media reviews are focused contributions used to examine audio, photo, video, screenshot, or file-based material in a careful and documented way.

## Purpose

A media review should help readers understand what was reviewed, where it came from, how it was handled, what was observed, and what limits remain.

Media review is useful for EVP clips, ITC sessions, photographs, video captures, screenshots, spectrograms, and other evidence-related files.

## Expected Style

Write in a restrained, evidence-conscious voice. The goal is not to prove a claim from one clip or image. The goal is to preserve context, document review conditions, identify possible contamination, and describe what can or cannot be determined.

The tone should be:

- clear
- cautious
- documented
- specific
- honest about uncertainty
- careful with interpretation

Avoid telling readers what they must hear or see. If a clip is ambiguous, say so.

## Recommended Structure

A media review may include:

- Title
- Media type being reviewed
- Source, date, and file context
- Original-file status
- Review method
- Observations
- Possible contamination or ordinary explanations
- Classification
- Limitations
- Conclusion
- Author note

## Media and Supporting Material

Media reviews may include:

- original file links when allowed
- embedded audio or video
- still frames
- screenshots
- waveform or spectrogram images
- file metadata
- timestamps
- related field notes
- equipment settings

Media should be labeled clearly. Readers should understand what they are seeing or hearing, how it was reviewed, and what context may affect interpretation.

## Classification Language

Useful classifications may include:

- explained
- likely explained
- unresolved
- unusable
- contamination suspected
- requires further review

Avoid treating unresolved material as proof. Unresolved means the material was not explained from the available information.

## Good Fit Examples

- Review of an EVP clip with timestamp and field notes
- Review of a photograph with possible reflection or motion blur
- Review of a video anomaly with before-and-after context
- Comparison of an original audio file and an edited sample
- Documentation of a suspected contamination source

## What To Avoid

- Posting clipped media without context
- Telling readers what to hear before they listen
- Cropping out important before-and-after material
- Presenting compression artifacts as evidence
- Making claims stronger than the file context allows
