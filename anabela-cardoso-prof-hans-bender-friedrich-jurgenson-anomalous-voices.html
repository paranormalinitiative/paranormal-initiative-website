Introduction

The painter and film producer Friedrich Jiirgenson, living in Sweden, was the first to report (in 1959) extraordinary voice phenomena on audiotape which he interprets as 'messages from the deceased’. In exploratory experiments, the Freiburg Institute in 1964 investigated, as a first approach to the question, whether this voice phenomenon is normally explicable or attributable to paranormal influence. A second investigation in May 1970, with improved technical equipment, showed the paranormal source of some voice phenomena as very probable. Analyses with the Visible Speech Technique greatly helped to objectify comprehension of the content.

I. Since 19638 the Freiburg Institute has been connected with Friedrich Jiirgenson, the author of the book Sprechfunk mit Verstorbenen (Freiburg 1967)0] who lives in Mé6lnbo near Stockholm. After a colourful life of education and occupation (studies at art academies and music colleges, opera singer, painter) the man from the Baltic who was born in Odessa in 1908, took up residence in Sweden in 1943. There he works as a visual artist and acquired renown in the last decade as a producer of documentaries preferably with themes about his adopted country, Italy. His works about the preservation of Pompeii, which is his special interest, are well known.

In the summer of 1959 Jiirgenson made an odd observation: after recording bird-songs in the countryside he perceived during playback a soft male voice, speaking in Norwegian about "nocturnal birdcalls". A series of "cackling, whistling and burbling noises" subsequently occurred in which he believed he could discern the call of a bittern. He contemplated the possibility that under certain circumstances a tape recorder could operate as a radio receiver. But he was concerned that ‘Just me, who was in search of birdsongs, Norwegian night bird calls were sent on tape just at that moment when I plugged in the device?’ (p. 19).

How it continued Jiirgenson reported in full detail in his book. During the following months he received on his tape recorder with a microphone, voice phenomena giving him personal messages. During

The role played by the projection of a preconceived meaning into an acoustic signal for its intelligibility remained an open question.

Because of a range of newspaper reports and finally because of his book, published in Swedish and German, the voice phenomena became widely known. Jiirgenson's thoroughly uncompromising missionary idea of 'radiotelephony with the deceased’ inspired a lot of people with hope for a communication with the beyond. As in Jiirgenson's case, words from different languages were often put together in one phrase, causing particular difficulties for understanding.

I. Ina first step the Northeim exploratory experiments served the purpose of clarifying the problem. There was no doubt that on completely new tapes which excluded fraudulent manipulations - and that was, anyway, extremely improbable considering the honourable character of Jiirgenson - acoustic occurrences happened, the origin of which could not be determined. The preliminary study or the discussion of hypotheses restricted itself to microphone recordings, because the clarification of the so-called 'radio recording’ has to take into account the difficulties to differentiate normal radio transmissions, or their fragments, from supposed ‘recordings’. Only clearly identifiable contents of personal information can give some guarantee of the correct evaluation here.

In the decision about the paranormal cause of the microphone ‘recordings’, the following counter-hypotheses have to be excluded:

a. Projection of an illusionary semantic content into normal speech or random noises.

b. Uncontrolled utterances of participants in the form of

CUADERNOS DE TCI 67

unconscious whispering or unintended interjections.

c. Fragmentary radio transmissions, recorded by a tape recorder working under special circumstances as a receiver.

To exclude the counter-hypothesis a), on the one hand, listening tests with a large number of trained participants are advisable, on the other hand 'Visible-Speech-Diagrams' as an objectifying technique, as reported by Sotschek. Naturally, listening tests are also necessary if there is an articulated acoustic occurrence whose intelligibility has to be established. All 'recording' [EVP] experimenters repeatedly demand permanent training of their auditory capabilities in order to be able to meet the special requirements of the alleged voice phenomena.

From the point of view of Dipl.-Phys. B. Heim, the counter- hypothesis b) at best can be neutralised by using larynx microphones for every participant. He recommends:

"If n participants are present, n+1 identical tape recorders have to be simultaneously controllable by one electrical accumulative switch. ‘To the microphone inputs optional n dynamic or n inductivity-free crystal microphones are connected, which are larynx microphones. ‘The remaining device gets an accumulative microphone. This must be a dynamic or an inductivity-free crystal microphone (optional attachable). If recording experiments, with n participants and with such an arrangement in progress, are implemented, human failure could be eliminated by generating the difference between the recording of the accumulative microphone and the n recordings of the larynx microphones."

The counter-hypothesis c) can be excluded with most confidence, if the experiments are performed in a reliably shielded room. It is also possible to shield the single tape recorder with the attached microphone in an iron box.

Regarding all these suggestions, especially the inconvenient larynx microphones, it is essential to consider that the spontaneity of the experimental ambience, necessary for the success of paranormal experiments, will be at risk of being impaired.

IV. For the time being, at the Freiburg Institute, further investigations of the complex voice phenomenon had to be held in abeyance because there was little chance of raising the required funds. Meanwhile ‘paranormal voices' became an internationally discussed curiosity. Early in May 1970, a surprising opportunity opened up, with Jiirgenson performing new experiments in Nysund. Participants in this improvised undertaking were the author, Dr. J. Keil (Institute of Psychology, University of Tasmania), philosophy student L. Bélanger and Engineer N. Lemke with his wife (Munich). During the period of the 5th to the 8th of May 1970, in a living room of the house of our host, 25 single experiments with microphone recordings were performed, with a talk time of 4 to 10 minutes. The participants sat around a round table, with Jiirgenson a little aside at his tape recorder (Uher Report 4000).

The following arrangements had been made: in front of every participant at the table a microphone was located, to which, at the beginning of the 12th experiment, a directional aid in form of a 80 cm long cardboard tube was attached. All the way through, high-class moving coil microphones were used. A ‘universal microphone’ hung over the table, connected to Jiirgenson’s tape recorder. The table microphones were connected separately with the two channels of an Uher-Report 4004 stereo device and an Uher Variochord 263. The microphone inputs of the tape recorders were protected by a broadcast lock, the microphone cables were kept short (maximum of 2.5m). Further, a Shibaden video recorder with its own omni-

Before use, the new tapes were rewound unto new metal coils to discharge them statically and to be sure that on the recording heads no sound was induced by static electricity. The room where the experiments took place was tested for strong radio stations and radiotelephony with a ferrite rod by Eng. Lemke and with a receiver we had brought with us. Repeated verifications gave negative results every single time, so that Eng. Lemke completely precluded counter- hypothesis c): fragmentary radio transmissions as an explanation for ‘recordings’.

Always after the conclusion of a test conversation, the recordings were listened to firstly from Jiirgenson's Uher device. The host, temperamentally driven also after many years of experimenting, called attention to supposed ‘'recordings', and the other participants tried to recall what they had understood. The participants were able to decide on 17 more or less clear 'recordings' - with some reservations. In comparison to this there was often a greater number, which Jiirgenson told us of during a subsequent audit of his tapes. At this time they are still not verified in detail.

Sotschek, member of the research team 'Acustik' of the Telecommunication Techniques Central Office, Berlin, and author of the following “ article, audibility measurements were performed with 5 ‘recordings’ (TANNER, TANNER; VON WO, ABER ZUSS; RASMUS and STOP - AS YOU LIKE ONE PAUSE). The Experimental ‘listeners’ were 7 members of an ‘audio squad' collaborating with the research team and 6 ‘listeners’ without experience in speech discrimination measurements. Language skills in English existed at different levels.

The questionnaires prepared by the Freiburg Institute initially allowed for a free interpretation and then a ‘multiple-choice’ rating, where the ‘correct’ meaning of every ‘recording’ should be found from 6 alternatives, which were all phonetically very similar. The interpretation for which the highest probability was determined after the session in Nysund and during the exact analysis in the Freiburg Institute, was considered as the right one. Every ‘recording’ was presented at least 20 times.

Result: In the case of the free interpretation not once was agreement reached in the evaluation. Often only single noises were understood and no coherent words.

The evaluation of the 'multiple-choice' ratings showed that three of the 5 'recordings' were properly identified with statistically significant majority. This is the matter of the 'recordings' "von wo" (54% correct), "aber ziiss" (46% correct), "stop - as you like - one pause" (62% correct). Indeed the percentages are far from 100, but the remaining 8 alternatives achieved far smaller approval ratings. (According to the binomial distribution of 13 classifications and 9 alternatives a percentage of 38% on the 5% level is already significant).

Among the remaining two ‘recordings’ was the interpretation, defined as correct, of a proper name unknown to the classifiers ("Rasmus'; "Tanner, Tanner"). That is the reason why here the interpretation was naturally more difficult and the number of correct classifications accordingly small. Nevertheless, at the free interpretation of the word "Tanner" some quite similar syllables were stated like "thunder, thunder’, but never the correct wording. However, also at the free interpretation, the word "Rasmus" was entirely misunderstood and was identified e.g., as "horch noch" or "hatte".

Comparing the results of the [personal] classifications with the Visible-Speech analysis, it is noticeable that the greater the differences in the free interpretation tests, the less precise the Visible-Speech analyses are. The letters that can be identified by the analysis, on the whole occur also in the interpretations of the [free] ‘listeners’, which are usually very different. For instance, one of the clearest 'recordings' ("von wo') has been freely interpreted as "wa go / war o / war so / bravo / wang ho". In his paper Sotschek comments on the results of the audibility measurements. He shows the difficulties that result from the usually inconvenient distance between useful and disturbing signal (the 'signal- to-noise ratio’) in the ‘recordings’. It is possible to offer the suggestion that the experimenters in voice phenomena have evolved special

Various possible working points (including electronics, tape head etc.) could be examined simultaneously by usage of adequate numerous devices.

For paranormal research the most pressing problem is most likely the awareness of the paranormal origin of certain 'recordings' and the clarification of the working point of the supposed psychokinetic influence. The psychological interpretation, in particular also the reaction to the spiritist supposition, is a cura posterior.

Summary

The analysis of extraordinary voice-phenomena on tape- recordings. Pilot studies on the «play-back-phenomena» of Friedrich Jtirgenson.

Since 1959 the Swedish painter and film producer Friedrich Jiirgenson reported extraordinary voice-phenomena on tape; he was the first to expose such findings. He interprets these voices as "messages from the dead". As an initial approach, the Freiburg Institute in 1964 made an exploratory experiment to determine whether the voice phenomena were normally explainable or evidence of paranormal effects. A second examination with better technical equipment in May 1970 made the paranormal hypothesis of the origin of the voice-phenomena highly probable. The analysis of the sounds with Visible-Speech-Diagrams proved to be very helpful for the objective documentation of the supposed meaning.
