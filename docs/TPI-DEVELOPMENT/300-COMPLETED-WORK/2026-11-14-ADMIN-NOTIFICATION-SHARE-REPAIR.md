# Administration, Notification, and Share Repair

## Incident record

- **Date:** 2026-11-14
- **Time recorded:** 12:50:59 EST
- **Reported by:** Todd Wayne, Owner/Founder/CEO
- **Scope:** Member navigation, Administration Center access, member notifications, community sharing
- **Release principle:** Fix the affected website surfaces without changing StudioFlow, Nova Editorial Workspace, Creator Studio, or other protected applications.

## What happened

Three site-wide problems were reported after the member-navigation update:

1. The Owner could reach the Administration Center route but received the restricted-access gate.
2. In Google Chrome, opening a community notification did not reliably take the member to the post that generated it.
3. Google Chrome did not show the same in-site Share menu that Firefox showed for Community Feed posts.

The preceding navigation change also took 31 minutes and 43 seconds, which confirmed that release scope and browser cache consistency needed to remain part of the repair record.

## Root causes

### Administration Center

The server page gate performed a separate direct lookup of `staff_role_appointments` before checking the account's effective role. That could deny an Owner when an older or unusual appointment record was present, even though `/api/auth/me` and the protected administrative APIs resolved the account as authorized.

The member shell also preferred a non-empty staff identity over the server's explicit `adminAccess` flag. That could hide the Administration Center link when the server had already authorized the account.

### Community notifications

New community notifications already carried an exact destination in the form `/member-home?member=1#post-{postId}`. The browser handler rendered that destination as a button, waited for the local readiness check, waited for the read-status request, and only then assigned the destination. That made navigation depend on JavaScript readiness and a successful asynchronous request.

### Community sharing

The Community Feed used `navigator.share` whenever the browser exposed it. Chrome could therefore bypass the TPI in-page Share menu and open a browser or operating-system share surface, while Firefox showed the TPI modal.

## Fix applied

### Administration Center

- The page gate now uses the shared `effectiveStaffRole` resolver.
- Owner, Executive, Full Administrator, and Administrator roles remain authorized server-side.
- Editorial Review and Editorial Publish capabilities remain separate fallback access paths.
- The member shell now honors the server's `adminAccess` flag in addition to compatibility role fields.
- Regular members remain unable to see or enter the Administration Center.

### Notifications

- Community notification Open actions are now normal links to preserve browser-native navigation.
- Marking a notification read is best-effort and runs without blocking the destination.
- Exact post anchors remain generated by the server and are honored by the Community Feed, which loads and focuses the requested post.

### Sharing

- Community Feed Share now opens the TPI Share modal first in every browser.
- Copy Link, Facebook, X, Email, and Close remain available through the same in-site interface.

### Cache and verification

- Shared `member-shell.js` references were advanced to `v63`.
- Shared `member-login.js` references were advanced to `v48`.
- Added `npm run verify:member-interactions`.
- Existing sidebar, staff-role, local authentication, release-impact, and shared-cache checks remain part of verification.

## Future reference

If a future repair shows any of these symptoms, check this record first:

- Owner sees the Administration Center restricted gate.
- Notification Open does nothing or stays on the notification page.
- Chrome shows a native share sheet or no TPI Share menu while Firefox shows the modal.
- A shared JavaScript change appears not to reach one browser.

The first checks should be effective role resolution, whether the destination is a real link with an exact post anchor, whether the action is blocked by an asynchronous readiness check, and whether the affected shared asset has a current cache version.
