Types of Hauntings and Claim Categories

A classification paper for organizing reports without forcing conclusions.

Investigation Science Historical and Cultural Research

Abstract

This paper treats haunting types as claim categories rather than confirmed causes. Classification helps organize reports, but it should not be used to declare what a location is before evidence has been reviewed.

Field Context

Investigators encounter reports of apparitions, sounds, object movement, emotional impressions, recurring events, intelligent responses, residual patterns, and religious or demonic framing. These labels come from folklore, witness language, paranormal media, and team tradition.

Motivation and Bias

Category labels can become conclusions too quickly. Once a team calls a case intelligent, residual, poltergeist, or demonic, later observations may be forced into that frame. Classification should reduce confusion, not create certainty.

Applied Method

Record the witness language first, then translate it into neutral claim categories for review. Each event should keep its own timestamp, location, conditions, possible sources, supporting media, and confidence level. Reclassify only when new evidence justifies it.

Conclusion

Claim categories are useful filing tools. They are not proof of cause. The most responsible classification is provisional, evidence-aware, and open to revision.
