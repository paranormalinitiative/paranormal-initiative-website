Introduction to Audio, Photo, and Video Review

A media-review paper on original-file preservation, contamination control, careful interpretation, and evidence integrity.

Evidence Science and Analysis Reporting and Documentation

Abstract

This paper outlines a restrained approach to audio, photo, and video review. Media files become evidence only when their source, context, integrity, and limitations remain intact.

Field Context

A single investigation can produce hours of audio, many photographs, and long video files. These files may contain handling noise, compression artifacts, reflections, voices from team members, radio contamination, device noise, or ordinary environmental sounds.

Motivation and Bias

Review is vulnerable to suggestion. If listeners are told what a clip says, they often hear it. If viewers expect a figure, they may find one in shadow or blur. Blind review and careful labeling help reduce this effect.

Applied Method

Preserve originals, work from copies, keep timestamps, compare against field notes, review the minutes before and after a claimed event, document contamination, and classify findings as explained, likely explained, unresolved, unusable, or requiring further review.

Conclusion

Media review should slow claims down. A clip or image is strongest when its context survives and its limitations are stated clearly. Unresolved media is not proof; it is material for further analysis.
