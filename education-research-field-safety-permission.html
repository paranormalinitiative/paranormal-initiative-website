Basic Field Safety and Permission

An ethics and methodology paper on access, risk, property respect, and investigator responsibility.

Ethics and Professional Standards Investigation Science

Abstract

This paper places safety and permission at the front of paranormal fieldwork. Legal access, physical safety, and ethical consent determine whether an investigation should occur at all.

Field Context

Field sites may include private homes, businesses, cemeteries, historic buildings, public areas, or abandoned-looking properties that still have owners. Each site has access rules, hazards, privacy concerns, and people who may be affected by the investigation.

Motivation and Bias

Excitement can make a location seem worth the risk. Teams may also treat verbal access as enough because they want the case to happen. That motivation can override caution unless go/no-go standards are written before arrival.

Applied Method

Obtain written permission when possible, confirm who controls the property, document allowed areas, assess structural and environmental hazards, plan emergency contact, set team check-ins, and cancel or limit fieldwork when conditions are unsafe.

Conclusion

No evidence is worth trespass, injury, or broken trust. Permission and safety are not paperwork around the investigation. They are part of the evidence standard itself.
