What Is Paranormal Investigation?

A field-method paper defining paranormal investigation as structured inquiry rather than belief confirmation.

Investigation Science Ethics and Professional Standards

Abstract

This paper defines paranormal investigation as a disciplined inquiry into unusual claims. The aim is not to prove a belief, but to document what was reported, what was observed, what ordinary causes were tested, and what remains unresolved.

Field Context

Paranormal investigation sits between witness testimony, environmental review, historical research, and media analysis. Field conditions are rarely clean. Homes, businesses, cemeteries, historic properties, weather, wiring, human stress, and prior stories all shape what can be observed.

Motivation and Bias

The main risk is entering the case with the answer already chosen. Belief, skepticism, client pressure, team excitement, grief, and reputation can all influence interpretation. These factors should be named as part of the case context instead of hidden behind confident language.

Applied Method

The applied method is simple: define the claim, obtain permission, research the location, document baseline conditions, record observations with timestamps, preserve original files, test natural explanations, and report the limits of the evidence. Claims and evidence remain separate throughout the process.

Conclusion

A useful investigation produces a reviewable record, even when nothing paranormal is confirmed. The stronger standard is not certainty. It is honesty about what was documented, what was explained, and what still cannot be responsibly concluded.
